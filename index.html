01 / An industry that has changed

Fewer farms. Bigger herds. A different milk industry.

Recent production has fallen, but the longer view reveals a different transformation: far fewer farms now sustain more milk than in 1990.

Key insight

Consolidation changes what “growth” means.

Between 1990 and 2025, national milk production rose by 32.8% while farm numbers fell by 75.5% and cow numbers fell by 21.4%. Together, these changes point to more output supported by a smaller farm base. The surveyed farm averages illustrate larger businesses, but describe a different population from the national totals.

Key insight

High yield and a large milk pool are different advantages.

South Australia has the highest output per cow, yet Victoria supplies about 63% of national milk production. Victoria’s much larger herd helps explain that contrast. Change also depends on the measure: from 2006–07 to 2024–25, Queensland had the largest percentage decline, while Victoria lost the most milk in absolute terms.

Key insight

Domestic consumption and overseas sales answer different questions.

The consumption heatmap tracks change per person, so it does not measure total Australian demand. The export flows instead measure money earned from products sold overseas. Read these as complementary views of dairy demand; their different units and coverage do not support a direct comparison of market size.

Key insight

More tonnes do not necessarily deliver more value.

Indonesia received slightly less dairy by weight than Singapore, yet its exports were worth A$96 million more. Indonesia’s higher average value per tonne explains the difference in their value rankings. This average reflects the products each market buys, rather than a price comparison for one identical product.

A smaller farm base, a changing dairy industry.

Australia’s dairy industry now produces more milk than in 1990 with far fewer farms, supported by larger herds and higher output per farm. Yet recent production has declined, and differences between states show that productivity alone does not determine the size of the milk pool.

Changing domestic consumption and demand across overseas markets add another layer to this transformation. Export volume tells only part of the story: the mix of dairy products sold also shapes the value each market delivers.
